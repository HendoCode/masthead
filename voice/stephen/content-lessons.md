## Seed lessons (from his stated preferences and edits to earlier copy)
- Open with a specific incident or task from his own work. Never with a thesis, a market
  statement, or a belief to knock down.
- Put the broader observation at the end, after the evidence has earned it.
- Write the quiet version first. If a sentence was chosen because it sounds impressive, cut it
  or replace it with the fact behind it.
- When a claim can carry a number, a date, a file, or a named system, it must. Adjectives are a
  fallback only when no specific exists, and then the sentence says the specific is missing.
- Match length to the ask. Stop when the piece is complete.
- Don't tell a company, or the reader, who they are. Give the evidence; let them conclude.
- Never let compression upgrade status. "The fix was..." must match what shipped.
- Before a post quotes a passing score, confirm the check could have failed (corrections log: a regression test that had never passed; a check that tested
  for the example instead of the category; an eval run that scored the environment).
- Every command a post shows was run as shown (corrections log: a published quickstart whose
  MCP server could never have started; settings that were never read).
- Similarity search is for meaning; exact lookups are filters (corrections log: a tool that
  summarized the wrong call, and a post that defended it).
